Factoring SEO Into IA Strategy

Search-engine optimization depends on two main ranking factors: how well keywords perform in a page's title, headings, and body text, and how readable that content actually is. A sound information architecture provides the essential foundation SEO builds on, if your categories and metadata already use terms meaningful to your actual audience: those are very likely the same terms people type into a search engine, so good IA and good SEO reinforce each other rather than requiring separate effort.

Nate Davis frames SEO-friendly work in three parts. SEO-friendly content means meaningful keywords appear naturally in titles, headings, and alt text. SEO-friendly programming means using proper semantic markup and human-readable URLs with consistent structure. SEO-friendly site architecture means meaningful navigation and contextual links that create real relationships between pages, along with consistent metadata use throughout. None of this involves keyword stuffing or other manipulative tricks: it's simply the natural output of doing IA and content work well in the first place.

SEO isn't a separate discipline bolted onto IA after the fact, when the underlying labeling and structure are genuinely good, most of the SEO work is already done.

Exercise

The scenario: A home improvement retailer's website has undergone an SEO audit, which flagged three issues.

1. Product pages use auto-generated URLs like "/product?id=48291&cat=7" instead of readable paths like "/tools/power-drills/cordless-18v-drill," and none of the pages use proper heading tags, everything is styled to look like a heading using bold text instead.
2. Related products are never cross-linked to each other, and category pages use inconsistent metadata, some have descriptions, most don't.
3. Product page titles read "Item 48291" instead of anything describing what the product actually is, and no alt text exists on any product images.
Chunk complete
Feedback

1: SEO-friendly programming. Non-human-readable URLs and missing proper heading tags are both technical implementation issues, exactly what SEO-friendly programming addresses.

2: SEO-friendly site architecture. Missing cross-links between related products and inconsistent metadata across category pages are both structural, relationship-level issues, the domain of site architecture.

3: SEO-friendly content. Meaningless page titles and missing alt text are both about the actual descriptive content on the page, which is precisely what SEO-friendly content covers.

Issue 3 is the strongest candidate for a minimum, high-impact fix, because it requires no structural rework and no engineering-heavy changes to the URL system or templates: it's fundamentally a content and metadata problem. Renaming product page titles from generic identifiers like "Item 48291" to descriptive titles based on the product name, and adding meaningful alt text to product images, are both changes that can typically be made through the CMS's existing content fields without touching the underlying architecture or programming.

This doesn't mean issues 1 and 2 don't matter, the readable-URL and cross-linking problems are real and should be addressed eventually, but they require more engineering investment to fix properly. Prioritizing the content fix first delivers meaningful SEO improvement quickly, while the more structurally invasive fixes get scheduled for when there's more engineering capacity available.
